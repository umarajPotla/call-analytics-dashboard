# Benchmark: rollup reads vs raw aggregation, and ingest throughput

Generated by `apps/api/scripts/bench.ts` on 2026-10-08.
Machine: 2 vCPU, 8 GB RAM (a small cloud VM), PostgreSQL 16.15 (Ubuntu 16.15-0ubuntu0.24.04.1) with default settings (shared_buffers 128 MB; the Docker stack runs 17), client on the same host.
**Treat the absolute numbers as indicative; the ratios are the point.**

## Dataset

- 500 accounts × 5 campaigns, 14 days. 10 large accounts at 20k calls/day (the DESIGN §11 assumption for an enterprise customer), 490 small ones at 1k/day.
- `calls`: 9,679,500 rows, 3049 MB with indexes.
- `call_stats_hourly`: 839,859 rows, 115 MB.

## What this says

- **Reads:** for an enterprise-sized account, the 7-day hourly chart from the rollup takes 1.1 ms (p50) and touches ~840 rows, vs 69 ms aggregating ~140,000 raw calls: about **63× faster**, and the gap grows with volume and range. That's why charts read rollups (D4), and why throttled refetching (D7) is affordable for a handful of viewers. With thousands of viewers it is still the first thing to break (DESIGN §11): the fix is a shared aggregate cache, not faster queries.
- **Ingest:** one transaction per event peaks at about **1,521 events/s** on this 2-vCPU database. The 500-customer design point is ~1.5k events/s at peak (§11), so a single small database just meets it, with no headroom. Batching rollup deltas in projection workers (§11, step 2) is the planned next step, triggered by exactly this measurement.
